* Summary

*Summary.* Across nine rounds (R15-R23) and 1019+ cells, R19's
K1-qwen30 hit *$0.00047 per quality unit — 51× cheaper than
K5-claude-solo at $0.02423/u*. K1 is the only variant on the FOSS
Pareto frontier in every round it appeared (5/5). The R18→R19
+20-point per-variant jump is mostly the worktree-mutex fix
surfacing real cells (R18 wiped ~64% of cells, R19 only 16%) — it
amplifies but doesn't invent the FOSS-vs-Claude advantage.
R20-R23 ran the round-chain autopilot but added no real strategic
delta beyond round-id renames; OpenRouter's $30 credit cap killed
all FOSS variants in R21-R23.

*Expanded.* Eight cross-round analysis agents + four R18-specific
agents fed this synthesis. Their consistent verdicts: K1 + K11 are
the FOSS workhorses, K2 hits raw-quality parity at n=25 but stays
6.7× more expensive per quality unit, K5-claude is the only variant
with cross-round CV < 0.30 (true stability), and K8-deepseekV3 has
an unexplained R19 regression that warrants a forensic walkthrough
before retirement. The single biggest missed lever is that the
R19 wiring patch added BK1-BK5 long-horizon tasks to ~TASKS~ but
the harness's Layer 1 is hardcoded to ~TASKS[0]~ and Layer 2 only
runs ~TASKS[1:8]~ — BK1-BK5 never executed. R24 must fix this.

* Cross-cutting findings

** The harness was the bottleneck, not the models

R18 had 90/140 cells wiped by worktree races. R19 dropped that to
~16% after the ~safe_worktree_add~ retry-on-collision fix
(commit fc59d1c). The +20-point per-variant FOSS jump R18→R19
was *the harness recovering real cells*, not models improving.

The remaining R19 noise (~22% bracket_imbalance) is being
penalized by ~quality_lint.py~ post-hoc, but the fix is
score-after-the-fact rather than prevent-emission. R20+ needs
N5 = pre-emit grammar gate, not post-emit penalty.

** BK1-BK5 long-horizon tasks never ran

The R19 wiring patch added them to ~TASKS~ but Layer 1 is
hardcoded ~b1 = TASKS[0]~ (line 1675) and Layer 2 iterates
~TASKS[1:]~ which only covers the original 8 tasks. The 5
long-horizon tasks (BK1 through BK5) are appended to TASKS but
the harness never selects them.

R24 fix: add a "Layer BK" that schedules BK1-BK5 against top-3
FOSS + Claude. ~10 lines of harness code. (See =scripts/
_round19_long_horizon_tasks.py= for the task definitions; they
are otherwise correctly formed and prefetch-tested.)

** OpenRouter $30 cap blew up R21-R23

After R19 + R20 burned $14 of FOSS data, the existing $30
balance was exhausted at 04:39 EDT. R21-R23 ran but every FOSS
cell got HTTP 402; only K5-claude (separate Anthropic billing)
scored. After the user topped up to $90 at 11:34 EDT, the
credit-watcher autopilot kicked in to re-run R21-R23 + launch
R24. (Re-run still in progress at synthesis time.)

** Modal preempted LoRA training; RunPod replaces

Modal's serverless A100-80GB pool over-subscribed. The K20-
foss-distill training got 1 step done in 10:11/step then sat
in scheduler queue for 5h. Killed; re-launched on RunPod H100
SXM 80GB ($2.99/hr secure) — bare-metal, no preemption. Pod
=jxgnvn01vaps1b= currently training; ETA 22-45 min wall, ~$1-2.

* The 51× headline — earned vs amplified

*Earned:* K1-qwen30's per-cell quality is real. Quality-judge
agent confirmed K1's R19 mean = 22.1 across 28 cells using a
quality rubric that catches the bracket-broken cells K6/K7
were producing in R18. K1 outputs are clean.

*Amplified by:*
1. *Worktree fix* — R18 wiped K1's score lower because cells
   died at ~git worktree add~. R19 had clean execution.
2. *BK1-BK5 nominally added but didn't run* — so K1's "score
   618" came from ~3-4× n on the original 8 tasks, not from
   the long-horizon torture tests. K1 has not yet been tested
   on a 5-file refactor. R24 will resolve this.
3. *Claude $/u rose 2.3× R15→R19* — partly because Claude burns
   more tokens on the longer briefs we added in R18 (Rule 2b
   skeleton, judge-fixes, survey-before-edit). K5 per-cell cost
   tripled R18→R19 ($0.10 → $0.37) on the long-horizon-style
   tasks even though BK1-BK5 themselves didn't run.

The 51× ratio is correct *for the tasks the bench actually ran*.
Whether K1 holds up on multi-file refactors (BK3, BK5) is an
open question.

** Dial ablation finding

496 of 562 cells ran the default dial =bt_pb_m1_tb_s1=. The 66
ablation cells are *only* on B1, and there:
- =bt_pi_m1_tb_s1= (inline prefetch) wins by +2.4 mean

B7 (where K7's noise stdev is 10.1, max 24 — best-of-3 candidate)

* Top three open questions

1. *Does K20-foss-distill beat K1?* The LoRA target is to
   improve over K1's R19 baseline by ≥3 quality points on
   B5/B11/B20 prose tasks. Same base model means the only delta
   is the fine-tune; if K20 doesn't win, the 81-pair distillation
   corpus is too small.

2. *Why did K8-deepseekV3 collapse R17 → R19?* (18.6 → 2.5
   quality). Variant-longevity agent recommends forensic
   walkthrough not auto-retire. Possibilities: broker
   re-pin to DeepInfra hit a serving regression, or our R18
   ~allow_fallbacks=False~ change cascaded after revert,
   or DeepSeek-V3's actual behavior on tool-format shifted
   over the bench arc.

3. *Will BK1-BK5 (real long-horizon) hold the 51× ratio?*
   The bench has not yet tested K1 on multi-file refactors
   where the model has to maintain plan state across 5+
   tool calls. Trajectory data from R17 Claude shows
   per-task tool-sequence length matters. R24 long-horizon
   layer is the test.
