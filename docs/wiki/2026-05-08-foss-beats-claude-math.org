* Summary

*Summary.* Quality gap is **1.0 point of 25** (Claude 24.63 vs Kimi
23.63 in R16) and it's localized to **one task: B11 DEC drafting**.
Kimi already BEATS Claude on B25 elisp by 4 points and ties on every
other R16 task. Four levers — three pre-launch in R17, one in R18 —
close the B11 gap fairly via better specs + atomic tools, not test-
tilting. Net effect: Kimi avg ≥ Claude avg, at 1/8th the cost.

*Expanded.* "Beating Claude" doesn't require a model breakthrough.
It requires correcting where the spec was thin (Rule 2b inference,
no-survey one-shot tendency) and enforcing where Claude has a
weakness (UUID fabrication). Same guardrails apply to all variants;
Claude does not get special treatment. FOSS already wins on cost-
per-quality by 8-10x — quality parity is the remaining gate.

* The 4 levers that close B11

** Lever 1 — Inline FULL Rule 2b skeleton REQUIRED-marked (R17 pre-launch)

Per claude-shape Lever B. Stop trusting "Rule 2b" by reference; inline
the exact section skeleton with REQUIRED markers in every B11 / B20 /
B45 brief. Format:

#+begin_example
REQUIRED HEADINGS (EXACTLY, in order):
  *Summary.*    1-3 sentences
  *Expanded.*   1 paragraph (REQUIRED — do not skip)
  *Context.*    bullet list, 3-6 items
  *Options.*    numbered, 2+ named options w/ prose
  *Tradeoffs.*  org table OR bullet list
  *Decision.*   one line OR "Undecided — open for resolution."
  *Rationale.*  1 paragraph (NOT "[to be determined]")
#+end_example

*Effect.* Closes Kimi's missed =*Expanded.*= ≈ +2 quality points on B11.

** Lever 2 — Survey-before-edit protocol (R17 pre-launch)

Per claude-shape Lever A. Add to B11/B5/B20 briefs:

#+begin_example
BEFORE editing, run:
  1. tail -100 <file> to find the anchor location
  2. grep -E '^\\** DEC-[0-9]' <file> | tail -5 to confirm
     numbering padding + short-title style
  3. read ONE existing entry of the same type as a style template
Only then write your edit.
#+end_example

*Effect.* Closes "one-shot tendency" failure mode where K1/K7 emit
something "good enough" on first try without consulting style. ≈ +1pt
on B11 for K1/K7, ≈ +1pt on K2.

** Lever 3 — UUID-fabrication enforcement gate (R17 pre-launch)

Per claude-shape Surprise S2 + synthesis Theme finding. Harness scans
every =[[id:X]]= in inserted text; if X is not in the workdir's =:ID:=
registry, edit is reverted with error message. Applies to all variants.

*Effect on Claude.* Claude's 1 fab/B11 cell becomes a -1pt penalty
(edit reverted, retry needed, and if the retry uses find_canonical_id,
correct lookup happens — but at cost of 1 extra iteration).

*Effect on FOSS.* Zero — FOSS already had 0 fabs in R16.

** Lever 4 — Worked-example: paste a real DEC verbatim (R17 pre-launch)

Per claude-shape recommendation §4 + synthesis Theme. Replace the abstract
DEC skeleton with an ACTUAL recent DEC quoted in full as the few-shot
exemplar. Pattern-match beats paraphrased instruction.

*Effect.* ≈ +0.5-1pt across all variants on B11. Most leverage on
weaker pattern-match models (K7-qwen72b).

* What this DOESN'T do

- **Doesn't make FOSS objectively smarter than Claude.** Claude's "5 small habits stacked" is real; we close it via better specs.
- **Doesn't generalize beyond the 8 R16 tasks.** Across new task shapes (R18's B30+, B40+), Claude may still lead until we test.
- **Doesn't handle long-horizon multi-file work.** R17/R18 tasks are mostly 1-file. Claude's reading-strategy advantage may matter more on multi-file refactors (R18 Track K).
