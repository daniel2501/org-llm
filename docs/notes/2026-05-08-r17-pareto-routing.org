** Verdict on K11

- *Aggregate cost ratio:* 34.2x. Aggregate quality delta: +14 mech
  (97 → 111). That's *+14% quality for 3320% cost.* K11 buys
  ~$0.20 per +1 mech-point relative to K1's free baseline, dramatically
  worse than even K8 ($0.005/cell at qual_per_cell 9.875).
- *Where K11 actually wins:* +9 on B25 (DEC entry creation, more verbose),
  +4 on B5 (but K8 also got 14 on B5 for $0.005). The B5 case is
  illustrative: K8 actually rewrote the gotchas table 30% shorter ($0.005,
  mech 14). K11's B5 work was cosmetic ("removed redundant 'below'", "is"→":"
  in 2 lines, $0.069, mech 4). K11 spent 14x more for less actual content
  change.
- *B11 outlier:* $1.57 single cell — single 28-line DEC-020 entry over
  171 seconds + 15 iterations. K5-claude-solo got 50 mech on the same
  task at $0; K8 got 36 mech for $0.015. K11 is 100x more expensive than
  K8 on B11 for *worse* mech.
- *Destructive check:* ZERO K11 cells trip deletions>100 in R17. The
  caveat about K7-R16-pattern inflation does not apply to K11-R17. The
  judge's worry was warranted as a check, but K11 is clean here.

*Final answer to "is K11 worth 35x?"* — *No, not at any quality bar*
*the data supports.* K11's mech advantage is small (+14% aggregate) and
*its quality is not 35x better; it's roughly equivalent.* The mech-leaderboard
position is an artifact of K11 being one of only 3 variants that ran on
all 8 tasks (and never erroring out). Per-cell, K1 and K8 are clearly
better $/qpt.

* Section 6 — Caveats + scoring notes

- *Mech vs quality:* the R17 mech leaderboard is destructive-edit
  filtered (deletions >100); zero K11 cells trip the threshold, but
  the threshold is a single-axis safeguard. A judge that scores
  *content quality* (not just diff size) is needed before any premium-
  tier choice can be made with confidence.
- *K5-claude-solo at $0:* Claude Code pilot session billing is
  external. Treated as a quality ceiling marker only; per project
  FOSS-only routing rule, NOT routed in production.
- *n=1 cells are signal noise:* 9 of 14 variants ran ≤2 cells. Don't
  read pareto-optimal labels as endorsements for these — they appear
  high-qual/cell because B1 is the trivial task.
- *Worktree errors masked 5 cells:* K2 (1), K7 (1), K8 (2), K13 (3),
  K16 (1). Fix the worktree-collision-retry before R18 — these
  silently zero out the variant's mech.
- *Scoring uses summary mech score directly.* Quality-adjusted score
  here only zeros destructive cells (a coarse cut). A separate
  semantic-quality judge run is the natural follow-up before any
  premium-tier routing decision.
